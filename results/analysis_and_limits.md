# Results analysis and limits

## Public outcomes

The confirmed best remains 0.9806. Adding split-trained V2 TTA at weights 18.75%, 25%, 37.5% and 50% to the earlier 0.9801 base scored 0.9803, 0.9806, 0.9805 and 0.9802. These results support retaining the tested 25% recipe as the best observed public candidate. They do not establish the optimal weight on unseen private labels.

Adding EfficientNet192 TTA at 25% to the 0.9806 winner scored 0.9805. Its standalone validation errors were 41 clean and 43 with flip TTA among 9,547 images. A separate split-trained proxy improved from 23 to 22 errors after a 25% addition, while NLL worsened. That proxy gain did not yield a public improvement. The new EfficientNet also differs from the original 128px run in normalisation, augmentation and split; it is not a pure resolution ablation.

The CatMouth/CatNose specialist had 18 clean and 15 flip-TTA errors among 2,082 true pair validation images. Frozen routing changed five all-class validation predictions: three corrections and two new mistakes, reducing the proxy's errors from 23 to 22 among 9,547 images. The submitted TTA policy changed 44 test labels and scored 0.9799. Changed test labels are disagreements, not known corrections. The policy was not selected by a threshold or weight grid.

Three earlier high-scoring submissions agreed on 29,314 of 29,443 test labels (99.56%). They share substantial ensemble components, so these files provide little prediction diversity. Agreement itself is not evidence that the labels are correct.

## Resize comparison

Notebook 09 reused the eight-class split-trained V2 checkpoint, selected at epoch 7. It compared the same images and weights with square stretching versus aspect-preserving letterbox padding. On original validation images, ordinary errors changed from 34 to 161 and flip-TTA errors from 29 to 154. The primary TTA change fixed one prediction and broke 126, including 116 new CatMouth mistakes and ten DogMouth mistakes. Fresh stretch inference reproduced every earlier cached baseline class label.

The secondary synthetic wide pass changed ordinary errors from 63 to 79; the tall pass changed 73 to 66. The favourable tall result does not outweigh the original-image failure. Keep the trained preprocessing for this checkpoint. This experiment did not train a model using letterboxing, and does not establish how a consistently letterbox-trained model would perform. No test inference or submission was generated by Notebook 09.

## Validation audit

About 69.82% of saved validation crops are near-square, versus 44.57% of test crops. A diagnostic rule using only crop shape predicts 2,067 of 2,082 true CatMouth/CatNose validation examples correctly (99.2795%). It has the same error count as the specialist but shares only one wrong image with it. This shows that the validation subset contains a strong crop-shape shortcut; it does not establish that the specialist uses only that shortcut or that it is the sole cause of the public-score gap.

The audit found no exact file or equal-size decoded-RGB duplicates crossing the cleaned train/validation or train/test boundary. The perceptual-hash search found 35 close validation-to-training candidates; four pairs were visually inspected and showed nearly identical views or overlapping crops. All 35 candidates were correctly classified by the reference proxy. Removing them changes its validation accuracy very little (99.7582% remains), so these detected examples do not explain the gap alone. The hashes can miss related images and give false matches. They do not establish source-photo or animal group separation.

The predicted test class mixture does not explain the aggregate square-crop difference in the diagnostic calculation. Predicted test classes are not true labels, so per-predicted-class statistics must not be treated as known test priors or error rates.

## Evaluation scope and completed DINOv2 experiment

The saved split 1337 has been reused for checkpoint selection and exploratory comparisons. Synthetic geometry checks probe specified transformations; they do not measure all forms of distribution shift. The exact 0.9806 ensemble includes full-data models and has no independent score on this holdout. A genuinely fresh confirmation requires newly training every compared model without its holdout images.

Notebook 10 completed a public-pretrained DINOv2 Base transformer at 224px using all eight classes: one classifier-only epoch and seven full fine-tuning epochs. Existing square resizing and mild augmentation were retained. This combines architecture, pretraining and adaptation changes, so it is not a pure architecture ablation. Epoch 8 was selected. The saved history records 143.1 training-plus-validation minutes on a Tesla T4, excluding most backup overhead and test inference.

Standalone clean/TTA errors were 24/29. The fixed 75% reference-proxy / 25% DINOv2 blend had 18 errors for either view, correcting five proxy errors and creating none. The clean and TTA blends have identical validation class predictions. Synthetic ordinary wide/tall DINOv2 errors were 45/40 versus 63/73 for the V2 reference. These remain reused-development and specified synthetic comparisons.

The submitted candidate used 75% of the exact earlier 0.9806 ensemble and 25% DINOv2 TTA. It changed 134 test class labels and scored **0.9806**, tying the displayed best. Neither the changed-label count nor the proxy improvement establishes better public or private performance. The displayed tie does not reveal the unrounded ordering. The clean variant was not submitted and has no public score.

The executed notebook, both backup archives, six prediction fingerprints, saved class reports and the candidate CSV were checked against the recorded inputs and saved probabilities. Checkpoint tensors were not loaded and no GPU training, inference or resumption test was rerun locally. Details are retained in `dinov2_base224/completed_run_review.json`. Public pretraining and staged adaptation were motivated by the [DINOv2 model card](https://huggingface.co/timm/vit_base_patch14_dinov2.lvd142m), [DINOv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193) and [linear-probing/fine-tuning study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054); those sources did not guarantee a competition gain.
